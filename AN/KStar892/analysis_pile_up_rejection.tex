The pile-up, or multiple interaction, study in the current analysis is based on the data from \href{https://phenix-intra.sdcc.bnl.gov/phenix/WWW/p/draft/dcm07e/DoubleInteractionAN/small-system-double.pdf}{AN1304} as well as frac (the fraction of the timing distribution within $+-5$ ns from the mean in BBCs) variable from the same AN. 

Due to high luminosities in light collision systems pile-up contamination is significant and cannot be ignored. Frac vs centrality ditributions (Fig. \ref{fig:frac_cut}) were investigated for possible rejection of double interaciton events. 
In \href{https://phenix-intra.sdcc.bnl.gov/phenix/WWW/p/draft/dcm07e/DoubleInteractionAN/small-system-double.pdf}{AN1304} it was shown that in each centrailty slice the tail of frac distribution matches the distribution of manufactured double interaction events, while peaks (high occupancy regions in frac vs centrality distribution) originate from single interactions only.
Therefore the centrality dependent cut with a function $\text{frac}_{\text{threshold}} = \text{max}(p_0 - e^{(centrality/p_1)^4} - centrality/p_2, 0.5)$ was introduced with parameters presented in Table. \ref{table:frac_cut_par}. 
Events with frac value lower than $\text{frac}_{\text{threshold}}$ were removed from this analysis. 
This cut removes most of overlapping events ($~80-90\%$) while removing only small fraction of events ($~5\%$ in \HeAu and \pAu, $~25\%$ in \pAl). 
$\text{frac}_{\text{threshold}}$ functions are presented on Fig. \ref{fig:frac_cut}.

Tails of frac distributions for different centralities were approximated in $\text{frac}_{\text{threshold}}/2. < \text{frac} < \text{frac}_{\text{threshold}} - 0.1$ region with 2 different functions (gauss and exponent + 1st order polynomial). 
An example of these approximations is shown on Fig. \ref{fig:frac_bg_fit_example}. 
These approximations were made under the assumption that no single event collisions forms the shape similar to the tail of the distribution since single events produce only one time signal in BBCs and thus have high frac value (which makes it impossible for a single event to have small frac value straightforwardly). 
However this assumption should be taken with a grain of salt as there was no analysis performed to determine this and in \href{https://phenix-intra.sdcc.bnl.gov/phenix/WWW/p/draft/dcm07e/DoubleInteractionAN/small-system-double.pdf}{AN1304} it was not shown what kind of shape single interactions produce. 
Therefore there may be a very small contribution from single events in the tail of a frac distribution, but this may just lead to a small overestimation of uncertainty, so not taking single events contribution into frac tail distribution is fine as there are much greater uncertainties in this analysis.

The frac tail approximations discussed above were extended in $\text{frac}_{\text{threshold}} < frac < 1$ region. 
This way background contamination was evaluated with contamination ratio - ratio of integral of backgroun distribution to the integral of the whole distribution. 
This evaluation was performed for $\text{frac}_{\text{threshold}} < frac < 1$ region for estimation of pile-up contamination after the application of frac cut and within the whole frac region for estimation of pile-up contamination before the application of frac cut. 
In the latter case in region $0 < frac < \text{frac}_{\text{threshold}} - 0.1$ tail of the distribution was used to obatin background instead of an approximation. 
But in this case this means that this estimate is a higher bound of a true value.

Values of pile-up contamination rate are shown in Fig. \ref{fig:pile_up_contamination}. 
Approximations were not performed in peripheral region since frac distributions become wider with larger centrality which leads to difficulties distinguishing the background from the peak.
However pile-up contamination in peripheral regions is always smaller than in central regions since multiple interaction often lead to higher event multiplicities which makes the determination of centrality of multiple interaction collisions heavily skewed towards central regions. 
Therefore central collisions contamination rates can be used as a higher bound estimate of the same value in peripheral collisions.
